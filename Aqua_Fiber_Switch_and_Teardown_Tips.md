# Aqua Fiber Tips

- Spools are dual cable - four strands, 2 x Duplex. Make sure you are unwinding both cables.
- Check both ends of the cable for damage before rolling out.
- Before you roll cable, check the destination to ensure it was not already run by somebody else. (Because of the below.)
- Fiber ends are often gaff taped to the spool. Preserve the gaff by loosely affixing it back to the spool. Don't fold gaff tape onto itself.
- The aqua-rolls go out from the main switch and the excess cable sits on the table next to the switch.
- Take special care when unwinding the first loop of the spool. It is easy to pull the wrong way and create knots on the spool itself, which compound as the spool unwinds. If you have difficulty doing this, get help. Some spools have "seen things".
- Keep the roll visible on the table. Otherwise someone might miss seeing it later.
- Don't tug on the fiber too hard.
- Don't bend the fiber too much.
- Jumper/patches and couplers are used to connect the spool to the switch. These may be gaffed or Velcro-tied to keep them organized. Give enough length on the big spool so you do not need to unwind the patches.

# Switch Tips

- There are wooden plates for each switch. Use them to prevent the switch from falling in between the tables.
- Distribute 2 power cords to each LAN table, for the switch & light. They are standard PC power cable and a DC adapter for the light, which might have its own power cable as well as the converter brick.
- LAN tables should have 1 surge strip with 6 receptacles. Make sure the power strip still has four available outlets. (Some of our strips are dumb and only have four to start; try to avoid using these.)
- Sometimes switches fail due to long term storage and their config getting corrupted. Sometimes they may fail due to hardware issues. Don't try to fix them at the event. Grab a good spare from the crate.
- MARK ANY BAD SWITCH BEFORE PUTTING IT BACK in the crate. There are silver sharpies and gaffer tape - probably on the main rack.
- There are **2** switches along each long row of tables. However, there are **3** on the row with the main rack and master switch. There is also one at streaming/theatre and those along the rental PC and nostalgia-zone tables. **TBD** Is there one at Help Desk also?

# Teardown Tips

- Half of setup chaos on Wednesday is caused directly by the condition of stuff we put away on Sunday a year ago. So, cleanup should be done right.
- Neatly re-spooling the fiber so it doesn't become a mess is important. **TBD** What is the proper technique to do this correctly?
- If our patch cables stay coiled, then there's little to do there. Re-coil if needed.
- Log what equipment goes where. Usually bins get labels so we know what is in them. Gaffer tape and silver sharpies are your friend. Slack is also useful to record one-off stuff as it happens.
- If there are spools that are significantly messed up in any way, note them in Slack so we know the number.
- If cable and switch issues are significant, we'll want to have a warehouse day to tackle them. Usually this happens in the fall.
